# Research gap and positioning

New Town has already been investigated for decadal land-cover and surface-heat trends (Regional Sustainability, 2024, DOI 10.1016/j.regsus.2024.100138) and blue-green UHI mitigation (Sustainable Cities and Society, 2024, article S2210670724008552). Therefore **LULC mapping or a heat map alone is not novel**.

The extension tests a narrower methodological and planning gap: whether spatially held-out validation changes the estimated skill of urban heat models, and whether recommended low-liveability hexagons remain priorities as indicator weights change. This precise combination is a proposed contribution, not a demonstrated first-ever method. Roberts et al. (2017, Ecography, DOI 10.1111/ecog.02881) provides the methodological basis for non-random spatial validation.

Key scientific concerns: the original pipeline reports only 21.21% agreement between its 2016 land-cover map and Dynamic World and 43.79% with ESA WorldCover 2021; 2016 TOA and later SR images are not directly harmonized. These weaken temporal interpretations and require independent labels and consistent preprocessing.
